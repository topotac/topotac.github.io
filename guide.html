GEOPDF Import — local incident and terrain maps

Import a georeferenced PDF

Open the upper-right layer menu and tap GEOPDF. In the GEOPDF IMPORT window, choose a downloaded georeferenced PDF, select a conversion quality, then tap CONVERT & SAVE MAP. Wait for the conversion status to report that the map was saved.

The NIFC PNW INCIDENT MAPS LINK opens an external incident-map directory. Not every PDF is a GeoPDF: maps without usable geographic reference information cannot be aligned and will be rejected.

TERaH (Terrain Extraction, Rendering and Handling) is the converter behind the GEOPDF feature. It processes the map in your browser and stores the resulting imagery on your device, rather than uploading the map to the TopoTac server.

Choose conversion quality

  • Standard 2×: Smaller output and less storage use.
  • High 3× (recommended): Default balance of detail, conversion work, and storage.
  • Ultra 4×: Larger output; use when the source map has detail worth preserving.

There is no 6× option. A higher setting can preserve more detail from the original PDF but cannot invent detail that is not in the source. TopoTac's map zoom limit remains 19; conversion quality and map zoom are different settings.

If conversion fails at the chosen quality, TERaH may automatically retry at a lower quality. Check the final status for the actual saved quality and tile count.

Display and manage saved GeoPDF maps

After conversion, the saved map is available as a named GeoPDF: overlay in the layer menu. Check or uncheck it to show or hide the map. Open GEOPDF again to see saved maps, tap ZOOM to move the map to its coverage area, or DELETE to remove a saved map and its local tiles.

The importer supports up to 3 saved maps and a total of 500 MB of saved GeoPDF tile data. Your browser or device may impose a lower storage limit. Delete an unneeded map before importing another if a limit is reached.

Field-use cautions

Saved GeoPDF imagery is kept in this browser's local website storage. Clearing site data, using another browser or device, or some storage-cleanup actions can remove it. Keep the original downloaded GeoPDF as a backup.

Conversion uses browser PDF libraries that may require internet access to load when first opened. Once saved, GeoPDF tiles are read locally, but other TopoTac base maps and live overlays may still require connectivity.

Verify alignment before operational use. Compare roads, terrain features, and known locations against trusted map data. A successful import does not guarantee that the source map is current or perfectly georeferenced.

Reading the radio coverage — common −116 dBm cutoff

Both offices display modeled coverage only at −116 dBm or stronger; areas below this threshold are transparent and the map lookup reports no predicted coverage. Each color represents the strongest predicted repeater at that location, not a signal-strength category. A colored area does not mean the signal is strong everywhere within it. More negative dBm readings represent weaker predicted signals; for example, −110 dBm is weaker than −90 dBm.

Check or uncheck GP COMMS and MFR COMMS separately. Both can be enabled, but their colored overlays overlap geographically. When both are enabled in an overlapping area, MFR takes priority for map-click identification. Disable MFR to inspect GP there.

Planning estimate only: Coverage comes from Radio Mobile terrain-based modeling, not real-time measurements. Antenna installation, vegetation, structures, interference, equipment and field conditions can change actual performance. Signal bars are illustrative, not calibrated receiver readings. Never rely on this overlay alone for emergency communications decisions.

Hose Lay Profile — pressure and available flow

Open and calculate a hose lay

Use DRAW LINE to trace a proposed hose route. Tap FINISH, name and save the line, then tap the finished line and select HOSE LAY PROFILE.

TopoTac retrieves terrain elevations and automatically recommends the lowest modeled Pump Flowing PSI that supports at least 10 GPM @ 30 PSI at every gated wye. The most demanding wye governs the AUTO recommendation; it may not be the final wye.

Enter a different Pump Flowing PSI or Pump Shutoff PSI and tap CALCULATE to use MANUAL mode. Entering either pressure estimates the other using the MARK-3 reference model at 10 GPM. Flowing pressure accepts 25–380 PSI; the initial field shows 125 PSI before AUTO runs. Shutoff pressure is a modeled estimate, not a measured setting.

If AUTO cannot meet the 10 GPM target at every wye, it selects the highest modeled MARK-3 pressure still supporting 10 GPM (approximately 346 PSI) and displays TARGET NOT MET. The dashboard reports the first wye below minimum flow; when all pass, it identifies the lowest-flow wye.

Hose length snaps to complete 100-ft lengths: 50 ft or less rounds down; more than 50 ft rounds up (723′ becomes 700′; 762′ becomes 800′). When rounding up, the last hose extends along the final drawn segment. Hose lays must be 100–10,000 ft after snapping.

Select DOGAMI (Oregon) for Oregon LiDAR elevation data or USGS 3DEP (nationwide). Outside Oregon, USGS is selected automatically. All terrain samples, wyes, and pressure calculations use 100-ft stations. Wait for the loading status to finish.

Internet required for elevation retrieval. If DOGAMI is unavailable, try USGS 3DEP. Missing terrain data may prevent a reliable profile.

Reading the chart

S = Static Pressure: Estimated no-flow pressure at the wye, including elevation changes. Negative values indicate pressure below zero (S:-12P).

AF = Available Flow: Estimated flow at the wye while maintaining 30 PSI (AF:5G@30P). Below 10 GPM appears red. Each wye is evaluated independently using the MARK-3 pump curve.

SL = Slope: Grade between wyes. Positive is uphill; negative is downhill (SL:-45%).

EG = Elevation Gain: Elevation difference from the pump. Positive is higher; negative is lower (EG:+167').

Hose Length: Rounded to 100-ft increments (50 ft or less rounds down; over 50 ft rounds up). All calculations use 100-ft intervals.

Calculations: Assume 1½-inch hose, gated wyes every 100 ft, and MARK-3 pump performance. AUTO recommends pump pressure to achieve at least 10 GPM @ 30 PSI at every wye, within pump limits. MANUAL uses your selected pressure. Downstream laterals and nozzles are excluded.

CHART SCROLLS HORIZONTALLY ↔

Swipe sideways or use the scrollbar beneath the chart to view all wyes and labels on long hose lays.

How the hydraulic estimates work

Each gated wye is modeled separately, with all other outlets closed. Available flow accounts for terrain elevation, flow-dependent hose friction, gated-wye losses, and the pressure/flow relationship of the MARK-3 reference curve. Do not add AF values together: the chart does not model multiple open wyes at once.

The model assumes 1½-inch forestry supply hose, a gated wye every 100 ft, 15 PSI friction loss per 100 ft at 60 GPM, and 0.46 PSI loss per wye at 50 GPM. Friction and fitting losses scale approximately with flow squared; elevation changes pressure by approximately 0.433 PSI per foot.

The classic 185cc MARK-3 reference curve uses 0 PSI / 98 GPM; 100 / 78; 150 / 65; 175 / 58; 250 / 38; 380 / 0, with linear interpolation between reference points. Flowing PSI determines available-flow calculations; modeled shutoff PSI determines no-flow static-pressure estimates.

Pump shutoff pressure is modeled. Actual required pressure may vary.

Warnings and field limitations

Minimum flow: A red AF value means less than 10 GPM @ 30 PSI at that wye. The dashboard and warning identify the first failing wye along the hose lay, not necessarily the most demanding wye used for AUTO pressure selection.

High static pressure: TopoTac warns when modeled no-flow static pressure reaches 350 PSI anywhere on the hose lay. This is a planning warning, not an equipment rating. Downhill wyes can have higher static pressure than the pump.

30 PSI at the gated wye is a planning target, not guaranteed nozzle pressure. Laterals, nozzles, multiple open outlets, water supply, actual pump performance, hose condition, valves, and pressure surges are not fully modeled. Sampling at 100-ft intervals can miss short terrain highs or dips.

Hydraulic planning estimate only. These results have not been field-validated. Confirm with field pressure and flow measurements and follow agency procedures and the ratings of all hose and fittings.

Line Construction Profile — production and time estimates

Calculate a proposed fireline

Use DRAW LINE, finish and save the route, tap the finished line, and select LINE CONSTRUCTION PROFILE. Choose a Type 1 or Type 2 hand crew or a Type 1, Type 2, or Type 3 dozer; select the fuel type and tap CALCULATE.

TopoTac requests USGS 3DEP terrain elevations at 100-foot stations along the drawn route, including the actual endpoint. A flashing red status message appears while terrain requests are in progress. Unlike the Hose Lay Profile, construction uses the actual drawn length, without rounding to whole 100-foot hose lengths.

Estimates use NWCG fireline production tables. Hand-crew rates use published sustained 20-person crew values and are not slope-adjusted. Dozer rates depend on fuel, equipment type, uphill/downhill direction, and 100-foot segment slope class.

Reading the construction chart

SL: slope between adjacent 100-foot stations, positive uphill and negative downhill. EG: net elevation difference from the line start. R: estimated production rate in feet per hour. T: cumulative estimated construction time, shown in hours and minutes.

Dozer production times may be displayed as a range because the published NWCG rates are ranges. Where a segment has an unsupported or zero rate, or a slope above the table's 74% limit, TopoTac shows an unavailable estimate rather than inventing a rate.

These are planning estimates, not guarantees of construction time, equipment access, safe operating conditions, or completed fireline effectiveness. Terrain elevation requests require internet access.

Draw Lines

DRAW LINE: Tap the button immediately to the right of ADD POINT. Tap the map to place each vertex in order; the line connects the taps. This is tap-to-place drawing, not freehand.

The temporary toolbar shows UNDO (remove the last vertex), FINISH (finish a line with at least two vertices), CANCEL (discard the unfinished line), and DELETE ALL LINES (remove all saved drawn lines after confirmation). DELETE ALL LINES does not discard the unfinished line or remove imported GPX tracks or marked points. A tip reminds you: Tap a finished line for Hose Lay, Line Construction, GPX export, or deletion.

After FINISH, enter a line name and choose Orange, Red, Blue, or Black from the color dropdown, then tap SAVE LINE. The completed line is saved automatically in this browser and restored when you reopen TopoTac. Repeat to draw more lines. See the Hose Lay Profile guide for pressure calculations and chart interpretation.

Small distance dots appear along saved lines at 100-foot intervals from the starting point, following bends in the route. Every fifth dot (500 feet, 1,000 feet, etc.) is slightly larger for easier counting. The dots hide with the Drawn Lines layer and disappear when their line is deleted.
The Drawn Lines checkbox in the upper-right Layers menu shows or hides completed lines without deleting them. It is checked by default.

Tap any finished line to see its name and length, HOSE LAY PROFILE for terrain and pressure estimates, EXPORT GPX to download that line as a separate GPX file, or DELETE LINE to remove it. Exporting does not delete the saved line.

Saved drawn lines are local to this browser and device. Clearing browser data or switching devices can lose them. Export important lines as GPX backups.

Marked Points

ADD POINT menu: Tap ADD POINT beside TOOLS to open its menu. Choose ADD SINGLE POINT, ADD BY COORDS, SAVE AS GPX, or DELETE ALL POINTS. Tap CANCEL to close the menu. The menu is separate from the TOOLS drawer.

ADD SINGLE POINT: Select this option, then tap the map once to create one numbered orange marker. The menu closes when placement starts, and the ADD POINT button turns red while waiting. After that single map tap, placement automatically switches off and the button returns to its normal color. To add another point, open ADD POINT and select ADD SINGLE POINT again.

ADD BY COORDS: Opens manual coordinate entry. Enter latitude and longitude in degrees and decimal minutes (default) or decimal degrees, optionally name the point, and select PLACE POINT. West and south coordinates are negative unless W/S direction letters are used.

SAVE AS GPX: Downloads the currently marked points as a GPX waypoint file. It does not delete them. The resulting waypoint-only GPX file can be reopened using TOOLS → GPX FILES → OPEN GPX.

DELETE ALL POINTS: Removes all marked points only after confirmation. The Marked Points checkbox in the layer menu hides or shows points without deleting them.

Marker balloon: Tap a saved point to view its coordinates in DDM and decimal degrees, copy coordinates, rename it, or delete that point. Drag a marker to reposition it.

Points are stored in this browser and restored when the map reopens as long as browser storage remains available. Clearing browser data can erase them. Marked points are separate from GPS tracks and imported GPX files.

Points and acreage measurement

MEASURE AREA / CLR MEASURE

Open TOOLS and select MEASURE AREA. Tap the map to place the corners of an area. Once you have at least three corners, tap FINISH MEASURE to complete the polygon.

TopoTac displays the approximate area in acres, draws an orange outline and translucent fill, and places an acreage label on the map. Tap CLR MEASURE to remove the measurement without deleting marked points.

Starting an area measurement cancels any pending single-point placement, so map taps can be used for polygon corners.

Calculated acreage is an approximation based on the polygon you draw. It is not a surveyed boundary or a legal acreage determination.

Coordinate display

Click Map for Coordinates: click a point on the map to display its latitude and longitude in degrees and decimal minutes.

Example format: 42° 26.208'   -123° 08.400'

Negative longitude values indicate west. The coordinate display is intended as a convenient location reference; confirm coordinates and boundaries against authoritative data when precision matters.
