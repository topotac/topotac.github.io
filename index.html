Understanding the chart labels

S = Static pressure (P = PSI): Negative pressures use a single minus sign after the colon, without a space (for example, S:-12P). Estimated pressure at the wye with no water flowing, including elevation changes.

AF = Available Flow: Estimated maximum flow at that wye while retaining 30 PSI residual pressure. For example, AF:5G@30P means 5 gallons per minute (G) at 30 PSI (P). Flow below 10 GPM is shown in red. Each wye is evaluated separately, with all other outlets closed; the pump discharge pressure can decrease as flow increases along the MARK-3 reference curve.

SL = Slope: Grade between adjacent wye dots, calculated from elevation change divided by hose distance. Positive values are uphill; negative values are downhill (example: SL:-45%).

EG = Elevation Gain: Net elevation difference at the downstream gated wye relative to the pump position. Positive is above the pump; negative is below (example: EG:+167').

Drawn distance snaps to complete 100-ft hose lengths: 50 ft or less rounds down, more than 50 ft rounds up. All wyes, terrain samples, and pressure calculations use the snapped distance. When rounding up, the final hose continues in the direction of the last drawn segment. Estimates assume 1½-inch supply hose, a gated wye every 100 ft, and a MARK-3 reference-flow limit. On opening a hose lay, TopoTac automatically recommends the lowest pump pressure for at least 10 GPM at 30 PSI at the final wye. If that target cannot be met, it selects the highest MARK-3 reference pressure that still supports 10 GPM and warns that the target is not met. You can change the pump PSI and select CALCULATE to use your own setting. It estimates the pump operating point and leaves downstream laterals/nozzles to the crew.

Planning estimate only. Automatic recommendation finds the minimum pump discharge pressure for 10 GPM at 30 PSI residual at the final gated wye, using terrain elevation and flow-dependent friction, and checks the simplified MARK-3 reference curve for feasibility. If the target is unreachable, the auto setting is limited to approximately 345.7 PSI (displayed as 346 PSI; at least 10 GPM on this reference curve) and the result is flagged. It does not model laterals or nozzle demand. Distance is snapped to full 100-ft hose lengths (50 ft rounds down); extending a line follows its final drawn direction. Each wye's AF estimates maximum available flow independently with all other outlets closed, holding 30 PSI residual and accounting for pump pressure dropping at higher flow. Assumptions: 1½-inch hose and one gated wye per 100 ft. Terrain comes from selected DOGAMI LiDAR or USGS 3DEP, sampled every 50 ft; small low spots can be missed. Actual flow, friction, nozzle demand, appliance losses and pump pressure can differ. Reference flow from 0–380 PSI uses the classic 185cc MARK-3 reference points; 380 PSI is approximate shutoff (0 GPM). 350 PSI is a planning alert, not a hose rating. Closed valves and water hammer can produce higher pressures.

Fuel model quick reference (Anderson 13-model system)

Choose the fuel model that best represents the fuels along the proposed line, not simply the dominant tree species.

1 — Short Grass: Mostly cured grass, little shrub or tree cover.
2 — Grass / Open Timber & Shrubs: Grass carries fire beneath scattered trees or shrubs.
4 — Chaparral / Mature Brush: Tall, dense, mature shrubs with substantial woody fuel; includes Oregon mixed brush.
5 — Young Brush: Shorter, greener shrubs with relatively little dead material.
6 — Intermediate / Dormant Brush: Older, more flammable shrubs than Model 5; less fuel than Model 4. Can include cured hardwood slash.
8 — Closed Timber / Short-Needle Litter: Compact needles and leaves beneath a shaded, closed canopy, with little understory.
9 — Hardwood / Long-Needle Pine Litter: Oak, madrone, or long-needle pine litter; generally more active surface spread than Model 8.
10 — Mature Timber / Heavy Understory: Heavier downed woody fuels and understory in timber stands.
11 — Light Slash: Scattered limbs and lighter cutting or natural debris.
12 — Medium Slash: More continuous debris from thinning, partial cuts, or clearcuts.
13 — Heavy Slash: Heavy concentrations of logging debris and large woody material.

These are identification aids, not a replacement for field fuel assessment. The calculator continues using the original NWCG production-rate model numbers.

Reading the chart

SL: slope between adjacent 100-ft stations (positive uphill, negative downhill). EG: elevation gain relative to the start of the line (positive above, negative below). T: cumulative estimated construction time at each station (hours and minutes). R: estimated production rate in feet per hour for that segment.

Crews: NWCG sustained 20-person crew rates; Type 1 and Type 2 direct-line values are used where published. For fuel models 7 and 11–13, NWCG provides separate older crew-type rates without direct/indirect classification. Crew rates are not adjusted for slope because this table provides no slope multiplier.

Dozers: NWCG single-pass rates vary by fuel model, dozer type, uphill/downhill direction and slope class. Segment slopes are rounded to the nearest whole percent before selecting a slope class, matching the SL chart label. For published production ranges, the midpoint is used for planning estimates (for example, 0–7 becomes 3.5 chains/hour). Only a zero-only rate or slope beyond 74% prevents a finite time estimate. No slope extrapolation is applied.

Distance: Line construction uses the actual drawn route, not the 100-ft hose-length rounding rule. Terrain is sampled at 100-ft intervals and at the actual endpoint. All construction slopes, production rates, and times use those same intervals.

Planning estimates only. Source: NWCG Fire Line Production Rate Tables (2021), pp. 2–3 and 7–8; original rates are based on older studies. Rates do not guarantee field performance. Crew values represent sustained 20-person crews, and dozer values represent single-pass construction. Weather, rocks, access, fatigue, operator skill, line width, vegetation, tactical constraints, and safety conditions can materially change actual construction time. Dozer slope classes use each segment-average grade rounded to the nearest whole percent, matching the displayed SL label; short steep pitches can be missed by 100-ft average slopes. No terrain data or unsupported production rate means no time estimate.
